C-ALL-001|all|First five minutes|Thank them. Coming out to you means they trust you.\pListen more than you talk. You don't need answers.\pAsk: "How can I support you?" or "Is there anything you'd like from me?"\pIf you freeze, borrow this: "Thank you for telling me. I'm glad you did. I'm here."|draft|2026-10
C-ALL-002|all|Keep it private|Don't tell anyone unless they say you can. Not other friends, not family, not "just one person who'd understand".\pAccidental outing is one of the biggest real risks. It can happen in group chats, tagged photos, a casual comment at dinner, or a "supportive" post online.\pAsk: "Who knows? Who shouldn't?" Then follow their answer exactly, even if it feels like a lot of secrecy.|draft|2026-10
C-ALL-003|all|Things to say|Helpful:\n"Thank you for telling me."\n"I'm glad you trust me."\n"You're still you."\n"What would help right now?"\n"Is it okay if I ask a question?"\pBest avoided:\n"Are you sure?"\n"It's just a phase."\n"But you don't seem..."\n"Have you told your parents yet?"\nAnything that makes it about you.|draft|2026-10
C-ALL-004|all|Questions: OK or not|Usually fine to ask:\n"What name and pronouns should I use?"\n"How are you feeling about it?"\n"Is there anything I should avoid mentioning around other people?"\pBetter not to ask:\n"How do you know?"\nAnything about their body or private life.\nAnything that asks them to prove it.\nIf you're curious, learn on your own. Don't quiz them.|draft|2026-10
C-ALL-005|all|Names and pronouns|Ask which name and pronouns they'd like, and use them. Practise on your own when they're not around.\pIf you slip, say sorry once, correct it, and carry on.\pIf you're not sure what to use around other people, ask. Some people use one set with you and another elsewhere, and getting that wrong can out them.|draft|2026-10
C-ALL-006|all|If you get it wrong|It happens. Say a quick "sorry, I meant..." and move on. A long apology puts the work back on them.\pDon't ask them to reassure you. Notice it, practise, and do better next time.\pIf you said something hurtful, the "I reacted badly" card can help.|draft|2026-10
C-ALL-007|all|You needn't understand|You don't have to understand everything to be kind. Listening and believing them is enough.\pGaps can be filled later, by reading or by talking to trusted groups, not by quizzing the person.\pYou can say: "I don't fully get it yet, but I'm here and I'm learning." That's honest, and it's enough.|draft|2026-10
C-ALL-008|all|Keep showing up|Afterwards, keep inviting them to things, as before. Check in now and then: "How are things?"\pComing out isn't one conversation. It's a long run of small moments. Notice if you start avoiding them.\pStand up for them gently when they're not there, and let them lead on what is shared and with whom.|draft|2026-10
C-ALL-009|all|I reacted badly|If you got it wrong, it can be fixed. Give it a little time, then reach out.\pTry: "I'm sorry for how I reacted. You deserved better. I'm glad you told me, and I'm working on it."\pDon't ask for forgiveness straight away. Let them decide when. Then show it by what you do: listen, use the right name, keep their confidence.|draft|2026-10
