I-TRANS|trans|Trans|A general description, not a test.\n"Trans" (short for transgender) is used by people whose gender is different from the one they were assumed to have at birth. Trans people can be men, women, non-binary, or something else.\pBeing trans doesn't depend on any particular surgery, medication, name or look. People change what's right for them, such as name, clothes, paperwork or medical care, or none of it. Medical choices are personal, and a qualified professional can talk through options.\pSome people know very young and some work it out slowly. Exploring is allowed, and not fitting perfectly is just being human.|draft|2026-10
I-NB|nonbinary|Non-binary|A general description, not a test.\n"Non-binary" is used by people whose gender isn't only man or only woman. It's an umbrella. Some people use words like genderqueer, genderfluid or agender, or just "non-binary" or "enby".\pNon-binary people may or may not change their name, pronouns, clothes or body. There's no required look. Some use they/them, and some use other words or a mix.\pSome feel their gender is steady and some feel it shifts. Nobody fits every part of a description, and that's just being human.|draft|2026-10
I-QUEER|queer|Queer|A general description, not a test.\n"Queer" is a broad word some people use for sexuality, gender or both, especially when other words feel too narrow. Some use it because they aren't sure yet, or don't want a specific label.\pIt was once only an insult. Many people reclaimed it, but some still find it hurtful. So it's best to use it for yourself, and to ask before using it for someone else.\pNobody fits every part of a description. Use it if it feels like yours.|draft|2026-10
I-QSTN|questioning|Questioning|A general description, not a test.\n"Questioning" means you're exploring or aren't sure. It's a perfectly good place to be, not a waiting room. You don't need to know, and there's no deadline.\pPeople explore in different ways: noticing who or what they feel drawn to, reading other people's stories, talking to someone safe, or small private experiments like trying a new name. You can stop or slow down any time.\pIf it's weighing on you, someone who works in this area can help, and the help menu lists people who can listen.|draft|2026-10
I-INT|intersex|Intersex|A general description, not a test.\n"Intersex" describes people born with physical sex characteristics, such as chromosomes, hormones or genitals, that don't fit typical ideas of male or female. It's about bodies, not about attraction or gender. Intersex people can be any gender and any orientation.\pMany intersex people have faced secrecy, shame or medical treatment without choice. Intersex-led groups can offer support and information. If this is your story, you decide who knows.\pThis is only a short description. For more, intersex-led organisations are the best source.|draft|2026-10
