Many men use it, and some people of other genders do too.\pPeople's experiences differ a lot. Some have known for years and some realise much later. Some feel certain and some are still working it out. All of that is ordinary.\pIf some of this doesn't fit you, that's just being human. I-BI|bi|Bisexual|A general description, not a test.\n"Bisexual" is used by people who are attracted to more than one gender. It doesn't have to mean equally, at the same time, or in the same way.\pYou don't need to have dated more than one gender, or anyone at all, to be bi. Attraction can shift over time, and that doesn't make it less real.\pFor some people bi is the lasting word. For others the word changes over time. Both are fine, and nobody fits every part of a description.|draft|2026-10
I-PAN|pan|Pansexual|A general description, not a test.\n"Pansexual" (pan) is used by people whose attraction isn't limited by gender, or for whom gender isn't the main thing. Some people use both bi and pan, and different people explain the two words differently.\pThat's okay. Choose the word that feels right to you, not the one with the "correct" definition.\pNobody fits every part of a description. Not matching perfectly is just being human.|draft|2026-10
I-ACE|ace|Asexual|A general description, not a test.\n"Asexual" (ace) is used by people who feel little or no sexual attraction. It's a spectrum: some feel it rarely, or only in certain situations.\pBeing ace isn't the same as being broken, afraid of intimacy, or choosing not to have sex. Ace people can want love, romance, closeness and family, or not.\pNobody fits every part of a description. If the word helps, use it. If it doesn't quite fit, that's just being human.|draft|2026-10
